# CFO Principles Compact Reference

## Core CFO responsibility

A CFO protects the company's ability to continue operating while allocating capital to the best risk-adjusted opportunities. This requires balancing liquidity, profitability, growth, solvency, compliance, stakeholder confidence, and resilience.

## Financial truth hierarchy

1. Cash reality: bank balances, restricted cash, undrawn committed facilities, immediate obligations.
2. Economic reality: margins, unit economics, customer profitability, lifetime value, production constraints.
3. Accounting reality: accruals, revenue recognition, depreciation, provisions, consolidation, audit trail.
4. Strategic reality: market position, capabilities, optionality, competitive advantage, externalities.

Never confuse accounting profit with liquidity. A profitable company can fail from cash timing, overtrading, inventory build-up, debt maturities, or customer concentration.

## CFO decision lenses

- Liquidity: Can the company meet obligations when due?
- Solvency: Is the capital structure sustainable?
- Profitability: Does the business model create economic surplus?
- Unit economics: Are transactions, customers, projects, or products profitable after variable costs?
- Capital allocation: Is each euro/dollar best used here compared with alternatives?
- Control: Can the company prevent leakage, fraud, error, and uncontrolled commitments?
- Risk and externalities: What can damage value outside the P&L line currently visible?
- Governance: Who is accountable, who approves, and who sees reliable information?

## Company stage differences

- Startup: cash runway, burn, product-market fit, fundraising milestones, founder discipline.
- Scaleup: unit economics, working capital, systems, hiring discipline, pricing, forecasting quality.
- Mature SME: cash conversion, margin protection, tax planning, operational efficiency, succession.
- Corporate group: treasury, cash pooling, transfer pricing, consolidation, governance, capital allocation.
- Distressed company: survival cash, stakeholder sequencing, covenant breach, restructuring options.

## Default analysis output

Use this format unless the task asks otherwise:

1. Situation summary.
2. Key facts and missing information.
3. Quantitative analysis.
4. CFO interpretation.
5. Options with trade-offs.
6. Recommendation.
7. Risks, controls, and externalities.
8. Next actions and owners.
