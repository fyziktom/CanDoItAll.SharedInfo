# Cash Pooling Playbook

## Types of pooling

### Physical zero balancing

Cash physically sweeps from subsidiary accounts to a header account. This creates intercompany receivables/payables and needs documented terms.

### Target balancing

Only balances above or below a target are swept. This preserves minimum operational cash locally.

### Notional pooling

Balances are notionally offset by the bank for interest calculation while funds remain in accounts. Availability depends on bank, jurisdiction, and entity structure.

## Risks

- Tax and transfer pricing risk.
- Withholding tax on interest.
- Thin capitalization / interest deductibility limits.
- Minority shareholder issues.
- Local law restrictions.
- Lender covenant restrictions.
- Currency controls.
- Insolvency preference risk if one entity is distressed.

## CFO design question

Before recommending pooling, answer: what problem are we solving?

- Reduce interest cost.
- Improve liquidity visibility.
- Fund deficits quickly.
- Reduce idle cash.
- Centralize control.
- Improve covenant headroom.
